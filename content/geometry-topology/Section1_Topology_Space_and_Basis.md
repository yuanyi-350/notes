**Def.** A **<u>topology</u>** on a set $X$ is a collection $\mathcal{T}$ of subsets of $X$ having the following properties:
(1) $\varnothing, X \in \mathcal{T}$.
(2) The union of the elements of any subcollection of $\mathcal{T}$ is in $\mathcal{T}$.
(3) The intersection of the elements of any finite subcollection of $\mathcal{T}$ is in $\mathcal{T}$.

A set $X$ for which a topology $\mathcal{T}$ has been specified is called a **<u>topological space</u>**

Example : 点集拓扑中的开集, 无限个开集的并集显然还是开集, 但只能有限交才是开集 (无限交不一定)

If $X$ is a topological space with topology $\mathcal{T}$, we say that a subset $U$ of $X$ is an **<u>open set</u>** of $X$ if $U \in \mathcal{T}$.

**Def.** Suppose that $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{T}'$ are two topologies on a given set $X$. If $\mathcal{T}' \supseteq \mathcal{T}$, we say that $\mathcal{T}'$ is finer than $\mathcal{T}$; if $\mathcal{T}' \supsetneq \mathcal{T}$, we say that $\mathcal{T}'$ is strictly finer than $\mathcal{T}$. We also say that $\mathcal{T}$ is coarser than $\mathcal{T}'$, or strictly coarser, in these two respective situations. We say $\mathcal{T}$ is comparable with $\mathcal{T}'$ if either $\mathcal{T}' \supseteq \mathcal{T}$ or $\mathcal{T} \supseteq \mathcal{T}'$.

**Example.** Let $X$ be a set; let $\mathcal{T}_c$ be the collection of all subsets $U$ of $X$ such that $X-U$ either is countable or is all of $X$. Then $\mathcal{T}_c$ is a topology on $X$

**Def.** If $X$ is a set, **<u>a basis</u>** for a topology on $X$ is a collection $\mathcal{B}$ of subsets of $X$ (called basis elements) such that
(1) $\forall x \in X$, exist a basis element $B$ which $x \in B$.
(2) If $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ which $B_1$ and $B_2$ are two basis elements, then there is a basis element $B_3$ which $x \in B_3$ and $B_3 \subset B_1 \cap B_2$.

If $\mathcal{B}$ satisfies these two conditions, then we define the topology $\mathcal{T}$ **<u>generated by</u>** $\mathcal{B}$ as follows: $A$ subset $U$ of $X$ is said to be open in $X$ (that is, to be an element of $\mathcal{T}$) if $\forall x \in U$, there is a basis element $B \in \mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in B$ and $B \subset U$. Note that each basis element is itself an element of $\mathcal{T}$ .

Check $\mathcal{T}$ generated by the basis $\mathcal{B}$ is a topology on $X$ :

(1) $\varnothing, X \in \mathcal{T}$ ; (2) Suppose $U_\alpha, \alpha \in J$ are collections of $\mathcal{T}$ , then $\bigcup\limits_{\alpha \in J} U_\alpha \in \mathcal{T}$ because : $\forall x \in \bigcup\limits_{\alpha \in J} U_\alpha$ , then $x \in U_\alpha$ for some $\alpha \in J$ and there is a basis element $x \in B \sub U_\alpha$. Therefore $x \in B \sub \bigcup\limits_{\alpha \in J} U_\alpha$ ;
(3) $\bigcap\limits_{i = 1}^n U_i \in \mathcal{T}$ because : $\forall x \in \bigcap\limits_{i = 1}^n U_i$ , then $x \in B_i \sub U_i$ for $i = 1,\cdots, n$. There is a basis element $B \sub \bigcap\limits_{i = 1}^n B_i$ and $x \in B \sub \bigcap\limits_{i = 1}^n U_i$

逻辑顺序 : 先有一组基, 然后产生这组基生成的拓扑. 因此知乎上"为什么基一定是开集"是平凡的问题.

**Example.** 基为所有平面上的开圆盘, 所得即为传统的点集拓扑.



**Lem13.1.** Let $X$ be a set; let $\mathcal{B}$ be a basis for a topology $\mathcal{T}$ on $X$. Then $\mathcal{T} = $ the collection of all unions of elements of $\mathcal{B}$.

**Pf.** Given a collection of elements of $\mathcal{B}$, they are also elements of $\mathcal{T}$. Because $\mathcal{T}$ is a topology, their union is in $\mathcal{T}$.

Conversely, given $U \in \mathcal{T}$, choose for each $x \in U$ an element $B_x$ of $\mathcal{B}$ such that $x \in B_x \subset U$. Then $U=\bigcup\limits_{x \in U} B_x$, so $U =$ a union of elements of $\mathcal{B}$.

**Def.** A **<u>subbasis</u>** $\mathcal{S}$ for a topology on $X$ is a collection of subsets of $X$ whose union $=X$. **<u>The topology generated by</u>** the subbasis $\mathcal{S}$ is defined to be the collection $\mathcal{T}$ of all unions of finite intersections of elements of $\mathcal{S}$.

**Def.** Let $X$ be a topological space with topology $\mathcal{T}$. If $Y$ is a subset of $X$, the collection
$$
\mathcal{T}_Y=\{Y \cap U | U \in \mathcal{T}\}
$$

is a topology on $Y$, called the **<u>subspace topology</u>**. With this topology, $Y$ is called a subspace of $X$; its open sets consist of all intersections of open sets of $X$ with $Y$.

**Example.** 将 $S^1 := \{(x, y) | x^2 + y^2 = 1\}$ 视作 $\R^2$ 上点集拓扑的子空间.

**Def.** Let $\left\{X_\alpha\right\}_{\alpha \in J}$ be an indexed family of topological spaces. Let us take as a basis for a topology on the product space $\prod\limits_{\alpha \in J} X_\alpha$ the collection of all sets of the form $\prod\limits_{\alpha \in J} U_\alpha$ , where $U_\alpha$ is open in $X_\alpha$, for each $\alpha \in J$. The topology generated by this basis is called <u>**the box topology**</u>.



**Def.** Let $\pi_\beta: \prod\limits_{\alpha \in J} X_\alpha \to X_\beta$ be the function assigning to each element of the product space its $\beta$ th coordinate,
$$
\pi_\beta\left(\left(x_\alpha\right)_{\alpha \in J}\right)=x_\beta
$$

it is called the projection mapping associated with the index $\beta$.inition. Let $\delta_\beta$ denote the collection

$$
S_\beta=\left\{\pi_\beta^{-1}\left(U_\beta\right) | U_\beta \text { open in } X_\beta\right\}
$$

and let $\delta := \bigcup\limits_{\beta \in J} s_\beta$. The topology generated by the subbasis $S$ is called **<u>the product topology</u>**. (类似外直积的定义)

**Thm 19.1** The product topology on $\prod X_\alpha$ has as basis all sets of the form $\prod U_\alpha$, where $U_\alpha$ is open in $X_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$ and $U_\alpha$ equals $X_\alpha$ except for finitely many values of $\alpha$.

**Thm 19.6.** Def $f: A \to \prod\limits_{\alpha \in J} X_\alpha$ by $f : a \mapsto \left(f_\alpha(a)\right)_{\alpha \in J}$ where $f_\alpha: A \to X_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$. Let $\prod X_\alpha$ have the product topology. Then the function $f$ is continuous iff each function $f_\alpha$ is continuous.

**Pf.** Let $\pi_\beta$ be the projection of the product onto its $\beta$ th factor. The function $\pi_\beta$ is continuous, for if $U_\beta$ is open in $X_\beta$, the set $\pi_\beta^{-1}\left(U_\beta\right)$ is a subbasis element for the product topology on $X_\alpha$. Now suppose that $f: A \to \prod X_\alpha$ is continuous. The function $f_\beta$ equals the composite $\pi_\beta \circ f$; being the composite of two continuous functions, it is continuous.

Conversely, suppose that each coordinate function $f_\alpha$ is continuous. To prove that $f$ is continuous, it suffices to prove that the inverse image under $f$ of each subbasis element is open in $A$, we remarked on this fact when we defined continuous functions. A typical subbasis element for the product topology on $\prod X_\alpha$ is a set of the form $\pi_\beta^{-1}\left(U_\beta\right)$, where $\beta$ is some index and $U_\beta$ is open in $X_\beta$. Now

$$
f^{-1}\left(\pi_\beta^{-1}\left(U_\beta\right)\right)=f_\beta^{-1}\left(U_\beta\right)
$$

because $f_\beta=\pi_\beta \circ f$. Since $f_\beta$ is continuous, this set is open in $A$, as desired.

**Example 2 (previous thm is not correct for product topology)** Consider $\R^\N$, the countably infinite product of $\R$ with itself. Def $f : \R \to \R^\N$ by $f : t \mapsto (t, t, t, \cdots)$ the $n$th coordinate function of $f$ is the function $f_n(t)=t$ which is continuous. But $f$ is not continuous if $\R^\N$ is given the box topology. Consider
$$
B=(-1,1) \times\left(-\frac12 , \frac12\right) \times\left(-\frac13 , \frac13\right) \times \cdots
$$

is open. Easy to check $f^{-1}(B) = \{0\}$ which is not open in $\R^\N$
